Executive Overview

Smart meters are a foundational component of modern smart grids, enabling two-way communication between utilities and consumers to optimize energy distribution, integrate renewable sources, and reduce operational costs. However, high-frequency energy telemetry introduces critical ethical responsibilities regarding user privacy, cybersecurity, equity, and informed consent.

1. Household Privacy & Data Granularity

Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring (NILM) algorithms can infer exact appliance usage and home occupancy from high-frequency metering data. Ethical deployment demands data minimization and privacy-by-design.

2. Grid Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Safety

Millions of networked meter endpoints expand the attack surface. Remote disconnect capabilities must be secured with multi-factor authentication, end-to-end encryption, and fail-safe designs.

3. Equity & Time-of-Use Dynamic Tariffs

Dynamic peak pricing models can disproportionately penalize low-income or inflexible households unable to shift energy consumption. Protections are needed to ensure social equity.

4. Transparency & Informed Consent

Consumers deserve transparent disclosures regarding data sharing with third parties, as well as simple opt-in/opt-out governance mechanisms for non-essential telemetry.
